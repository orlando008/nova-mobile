# Music Tutor

Music Tutor is a portrait note-reading game. Pick an answer mode and a difficulty, then identify ten notes shown on a treble staff. In Letters mode, tap one of three choices or use keys 1–3. In Keyboard mode, tap a white or black key from middle C (C4) through the top-line F (F5), with F♯5 at the right edge. A black key works for either spelling of its pitch. The results show how many notes you identified correctly, and you can repeat the round or change its settings.

| Difficulty | Notes per round |
| --- | --- |
| Natural notes | 10 natural notes |
| Sharps | 5 natural notes and 5 sharps |
| Sharps & flats | 5 natural notes, 2 sharps, and 3 flats |
| Key signatures | 6 notes read from the key signature and 4 notes with written accidentals |

Letters prompts span the bottom E to the top F. Keyboard prompts include middle C on the ledger line and D in the space below the staff. In Letters mode, each question offers three distinct names and pitches that fit the visible marks. The first three difficulties give all three choices the same accidental as the note. Key signatures uses a three- or four-mark signature for the round. It applies to matching letters in every octave. A written sharp, flat, or natural takes precedence for its question. An answer mode, difficulty, and seed reproduce the same lesson. A new round gets a new seed.

Starting a lesson enables audio in the browser. Each note plays as it appears, and tapping the staff replays it. Selecting an answer plays the pitch represented by that choice, followed by an ascending phrase for a correct answer or a descending phrase for a wrong answer. The selected button changes color and the text below it gives the answer. Use the mute button to practice reading without audio.

The cover and engraved flourishes are original generated art based on the Music Tutor concept. The staff symbols use rasterized glyphs from [Noto Music](https://github.com/notofonts/music), a typeface distributed under the SIL Open Font License. The plucked instrument and feedback phrases are synthesized with Web Audio.
